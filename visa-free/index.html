China visa-free entry: 50 countries, up to 30 days

Short answer: if you hold an ordinary passport from one of 50 listed countries, you can enter China for tourism, business, family visits, exchange or transit and stay up to 30 days without applying for a visa. You still need a valid passport (usually 6+ months), onward/return tickets and enough funds. Visa-free entry is not work or study permission.

Renewal window: most single-sided visa-free arrangements run to 31 December 2026. As of November 2026 the foreign ministry says it is studying an extension. Treat this page as valid for travel before that date, and re-check after any renewal announcement.

What to carry

  • Passport valid for at least 6 months with blank pages.
  • Confirmed onward or return ticket (often checked).
  • Hotel booking or host details for the first nights.
  • Enough funds; some travellers carry a bank card linked to Alipay/WeChat Pay.
  • Travel insurance (strongly recommended; not always checked).

How to confirm your eligibility

  1. Check your passport country. Confirm your country appears in the 50-country single-sided visa-free list above and that you hold an ordinary passport.
  2. Match your purpose. Visa-free entry covers tourism, business, family and friends visits, exchange and transit — not work, study or journalism.
  3. Prepare your documents. Carry a passport valid for at least six months with blank pages, a confirmed onward or return ticket and proof of where you will stay.
  4. Enter through an open port. Arrive at a port that accepts visa-free entry, then be ready to show bookings and sufficient funds to the border inspector.

Refusal triggers: visa-free entry is not a right. Border inspectors can refuse entry for incomplete documents, suspected overstay risk, prior violations, or missing onward tickets. When in doubt, obtain a visa before travel.

Frequently asked

Can I extend a 30-day visa-free stay?

Generally no. If you need longer, apply for a visa before travelling or leave and re-enter under the same policy where permitted.

Does visa-free entry let me work remotely?

It permits tourism/business visits, not employment in China. "Digital nomad" remote work sits in a grey area — do not assume it is allowed.

Is Hong Kong / Macau included?

They are separate immigration jurisdictions with their own rules. Moving between mainland China and the SARs counts as an exit/entry.
