China entry FAQ

Which countries can visit China without a visa?

Citizens of 50 countries can enter mainland China visa-free for up to 30 days under the single-sided policy — much of Europe plus Australia, New Zealand, Japan, South Korea, Argentina and others. A further 57 qualify for the 240-hour transit policy, and 61 for Hainan’s 30-day scheme. See our full country lists.

What is the 240-hour visa-free transit?

Nationals of 57 countries can enter through one of 65 designated ports and stay up to 240 hours (10 days) when travelling onward to a third country or region, provided they hold a confirmed dated-and-seated connecting ticket.

Can I work in China on a visa-free entry?

No. Visa-free entry covers tourism, business visits, family visits and exchange. Work, study and journalism require the appropriate visa.

Can I pay with a foreign credit card in China?

Yes. Link Visa, Mastercard, UnionPay, JCB, Diners Club, Discover or American Express to Alipay or WeChat Pay, then pay by scanning merchant QR codes. Some overseas wallets also scan directly at supported merchants.

Is Google blocked in China?

Yes, along with WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook and X. Use WeChat for messaging, Amap for navigation, and a travel eSIM or local SIM for data. VPN use is legally restricted in mainland China.

Do I need to book China train tickets in advance?

For Golden Week, Spring Festival and summer peaks, yes — popular high-speed routes sell out. Off-peak you can usually buy same-day tickets, but advance booking is safer.

What is the best time to visit China?

April–May and September–October suit most first-time itineraries. Hainan is best from November to March. Avoid the crowded 1–7 October National Day week unless you plan well ahead.

Does Hong Kong count as part of a China visa?

Hong Kong and Macau have separate immigration systems. Moving between mainland China and either SAR counts as an exit and re-entry, which can affect visa-free day counts.
