Foreign card limits and fees in China

Short answer: linking a foreign card works, but it is not unlimited. Per-transaction and cumulative caps apply, they differ by card and account, and the authoritative numbers live inside your Alipay or WeChat Pay app — not on this page. Treat anything quoted elsewhere as indicative only.

Where to read your real limits

  1. Open Alipay → Me → Wallet → Bank cards → Add bank card, or WeChat → Me → Services → Wallet.
  2. Enter the card details; the app displays the applicable binding and spending limits for your card.
  3. Screenshot that screen before departure and again on arrival — it can change with policy updates.
Limit types you will meet (indicative, not official figures)
TypeWhat it meansWhere it is set
Per-transaction capMaximum amount for one paymentCard network + payment app + your issuer
Cumulative capRunning total allowed over a periodPayment app
Issuer capYour own bank’s overseas/contactless settingsYour home bank app
Pre-authorisation holdTemporary freeze, common at hotels and rental desksMerchant

Fees

  • Currency conversion: direct foreign-card payments typically carry a conversion charge (commonly cited around 3%); confirm in-app.
  • Merchant surcharge: none — Chinese merchants do not add fees for QR payments.
  • ATM withdrawals: your bank’s own overseas rules apply, plus possible ATM operator fees.

Declined payment: fix checklist

  • Check the remaining limit in the app, then retry a smaller amount.
  • Try the other network (Alipay vs WeChat Pay) or a second card.
  • Confirm your issuer allows overseas online/contactless transactions.
  • Use cash or a Chinese bank card as fallback; keep some RMB notes on hand.
